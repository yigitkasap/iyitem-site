<p><strong>Interventional radiology covers diagnostic and therapeutic procedures performed with fine needles and catheters under the guidance of ultrasound, computed tomography or fluoroscopy. Because the operator works under imaging, the target is reached with millimetre accuracy and the result is achieved through a far smaller intervention than open surgery.</strong></p>

<p>Procedures are performed under local anaesthesia and, in most cases, the patient is discharged the same day. At our centre interventional procedures are carried out by our radiologists, using whichever imaging method the procedure calls for.</p>

<h4>Image-guided biopsy</h4>

<p>Fine-needle aspiration or tru-cut biopsy can be taken under ultrasound guidance from the thyroid, breast, liver, kidney and superficial soft-tissue lesions. Imaging guidance allows the needle's advance towards the target lesion to be followed in real time, which both improves sampling accuracy and avoids neighbouring vessels and organs.</p>

<h4>Drainage and therapeutic procedures</h4>

<p>This heading covers image-guided drainage of fluid collected in the abdominal cavity or the pleural space (paracentesis, thoracentesis) and drainage of abscess or cyst contents with a needle or catheter. The procedure both provides a sample for diagnosis and brings the patient rapid relief.</p>

<h4>What should you be aware of beforehand?</h4>

<p>Before an interventional procedure we need to know about any blood-thinning medication you take and your bleeding and clotting values. If your doctor considers it necessary, you may be asked to stop certain medicines a few days beforehand. Depending on the procedure, fasting may be required; the preparation specific to you is explained when you book.</p>
